# To: Priya Raman — Weekly support intelligence pilot

The support export points to a specific opportunity: **reduce repeat contacts from 13.1% to 10.0%**. On the tickets old enough to observe a full 30 days, 1,369 of 10,484 completed cases were followed by another contact from the same customer about the same product and complaint category. At your stated 650 contacts per week and the policy's Rs 290 blended contact cost, a three-point reduction would avoid about 258 contacts and be worth **roughly Rs 75,000 per quarter**. This is a planning estimate; savings depend on real contacts leaving the queue.

The weekly digest shows complaint categories, movement from the prior week, recurring wording in customer messages, repeat contacts, first-response breaches, and transfers. The strongest repeat concentrations are Delivery & Shipping (17.1%), Audio Quality (16.7%), and Charging & Battery (16.1%). I would start with those queues, choose one operational fix each week, and watch the repeat rate after the 30-day measurement window closes.

The requested weekly agent table is included, but it counts Tier-1 tickets by **resolution week** and is labelled as volume. The policy explicitly excludes Tier-2 warranty specialists from volume comparisons. Shift coverage, case complexity, and time off also affect counts, so this table should be used to ask questions, not make personnel judgments by itself.

Two cautions matter. The export has no issue identifier, so the repeat measure uses customer, product, and category as a proxy; it can overcount unrelated issues and miss cases that were retagged. The text themes are prompts for review, not verified complaint labels. A held-out text check disagreed with existing intake tags on 18.0% of tickets, and those tags are themselves imperfect.

The first pilot should review repeat-heavy delivery and product cases, log the operational change made, then compare mature 30-day repeat rates across subsequent weeks. The digest runs locally. A production integration can wait until this pilot shows that the weekly review changes decisions.
